# Food and Calorie Planning

Backpacking burns 3,000 to 5,000 calories per day depending on terrain, pack weight, body size, and pace. Most people eat too little on day one and pay for it later in the trip.

## Calorie Targets
A common rule of thumb is 1.5 to 2 lbs (700 to 900 g) of food per person per day, aiming for 100 to 125 calories per ounce. Below 100 cal/oz and you're carrying water weight you don't need. Lighter trips, shorter days, and cooler weather sit at the lower end; thru-hikes and winter trips at the upper.

## Macronutrients
- Fat: 9 cal/g — most calorie-dense. Olive oil, nuts, nut butter, cheese.
- Carbs: 4 cal/g — quick energy. Tortillas, instant rice, dried fruit, candy.
- Protein: 4 cal/g — recovery. Jerky, tuna packets, dehydrated meals, protein bars.

A working balance is roughly 50% carbs, 35% fat, 15% protein, but no one weighs this on trail. Eat what you'll actually finish.

## Sample Day (~3,500 cal, ~1.7 lb)
- Breakfast: Instant oatmeal + powdered milk + nut butter packet (~600 cal)
- Snacks while hiking: Trail mix, bars, candy (~1,200 cal split across the day)
- Lunch: Tortilla + tuna packet + cheese + olive oil (~700 cal)
- Dinner: Dehydrated meal (Mountain House, Peak Refuel) or homemade pasta side + olive oil (~800 cal)
- Dessert: Chocolate, cookies, instant pudding (~200 cal)

## Water
Plan for 2 to 4 liters per day depending on heat and exertion. In dry country, more. Always know where your next reliable water source is — apps like Gaia GPS and FarOut have water source data for popular trails.

## Common Mistakes
- Bringing only "healthy" food and undereating
- Forgetting how much fuel dinners need (test at home first)
- Not testing dehydrated meals before relying on them — some are inedible
- Carrying glass or heavy packaging
