# Water Treatment in the Backcountry

Backcountry water can carry bacteria (E. coli, Salmonella), protozoa (Giardia, Cryptosporidium), and rarely viruses. Treating water is non-negotiable on virtually all trips in North America, even from "clean-looking" mountain streams.

## Hollow Fiber Filters
Examples: Sawyer Squeeze, Katadyn BeFree, Platypus QuickDraw.

These pull water through tiny pores that physically block bacteria and protozoa. Lightweight, fast, no waiting time. Most popular choice for thru-hikers.

Drawbacks: Don't remove viruses (rare in North America). Can crack if frozen — sleep with them in your bag below freezing.

## Chemical Treatment
Examples: Aquatabs, Aquamira drops.

Lightweight, cheap, and reliable backup. Drops kill viruses too. Drawbacks: 30-minute wait time, slight taste.

Many experienced backpackers carry Aquatabs as a backup even when filtering — adds an ounce, saves a trip if your filter fails.

## UV Treatment
Examples: SteriPEN.

Treats one liter at a time with UV light. Doesn't work in cloudy water. Battery dependent.

## Boiling
Always works. One minute at a rolling boil kills everything (three minutes above 6,500 ft elevation). Uses fuel and time, so usually only used for cooking water or as emergency backup.

## What Most People Use
A Sawyer Squeeze or Platypus QuickDraw plus a few Aquatabs as backup is the standard combination for three-season trips in North America.

## Common Mistakes
- Drinking untreated water because it "looks clean"
- Letting a filter freeze (cracks the fibers, you'll never know until you get sick)
- Using a filter that's never been backflushed and complaining about slow flow
- Forgetting backup when relying on a single piece of gear
