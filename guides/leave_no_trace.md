# Leave No Trace Principles for Backpackers

The seven Leave No Trace (LNT) principles are the foundation of responsible backcountry travel. Following them keeps wilderness areas open, healthy, and enjoyable for everyone.

## 1. Plan Ahead and Prepare
Know the regulations for the area you're visiting. Check weather, permit requirements, and group size limits. Pack out everything you pack in. Repackage food to minimize trash.

## 2. Travel and Camp on Durable Surfaces
Stick to established trails and campsites whenever possible. In pristine areas, spread out so no single spot gets trampled. Camp at least 200 feet (about 70 adult steps) from lakes and streams.

## 3. Dispose of Waste Properly
Pack out all trash, including food scraps and toilet paper. For human waste, dig a cathole 6 to 8 inches deep and at least 200 feet from water, trails, and camp. In some heavily used areas like Mt. Whitney or parts of the Grand Canyon, you must pack out solid human waste using a WAG bag.

## 4. Leave What You Find
Don't take rocks, plants, artifacts, or other natural objects. Don't build structures, dig trenches, or carve into trees.

## 5. Minimize Campfire Impact
Use a stove for cooking. If you must have a fire, use existing fire rings, keep it small, and burn only small dead-and-down wood. Many high-use and high-elevation areas ban fires entirely — check before you go.

## 6. Respect Wildlife
Observe animals from a distance. Never feed wildlife — it harms their health and creates dangerous habituation. Store food and scented items properly using a bear canister, Ursack, or hang where appropriate.

## 7. Be Considerate of Other Visitors
Yield to uphill hikers. Keep voices and music low. Camp out of sight from trails when possible.

## Common Mistakes
- Burying trash instead of packing it out (animals dig it up)
- Washing dishes directly in streams (use a pot and dump 200 feet away)
- Building unnecessary fire rings
- Cutting switchbacks
